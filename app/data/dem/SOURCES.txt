Elevation crops in this folder
=============================

app/elevation.py reads these files (from this folder, or from BANJIR_DEM_DIR when that is set) to give the height of
the ground at a business or a picked square. They are small, so unlike the flood depth rasters they are kept in git.
tests/test_elevation.py checks each file below against its size and SHA-256. To make them again, from 11_Platform_Draft:

  .venv/bin/python tools/build_elevation.py fetch --name shah-alam --bbox 101.43 2.95 101.59 3.23 \
      --raw ../05_Raw_Data_Sources/copernicus_glo30
  .venv/bin/python tools/build_elevation.py build --name shah-alam --bbox 101.43 2.95 101.59 3.23 \
      --raw ../05_Raw_Data_Sources/copernicus_glo30

`fetch` asks Overpass again, so the rivers (and the files) can differ from the ones below; `build` alone, on the saved
inputs listed here, gives the same bytes (run twice on 4 Oct 2026). The method is in the tool's docstring.

Coverage:  Shah Alam, the box 2.95-3.23 N, 101.43-101.59 E plus 2 km on each side: 1139 x 705 squares of 1 arc second
           (about 31 m), west edge 101.4120833 E, north edge 3.2481944 N, pixel is area, WGS 84. Elsewhere the page
           says the height is not loaded for that spot yet.
Values:    float32 metres rounded to 0.1 m, 256 px deflate tiles, no data -9999.
           surface      the GLO-30 height above the EGM2008 geoid (about mean sea level): roofs and trees are in it
           above_river  surface minus the level of the nearest mapped river within 3 km (no data beyond: 79,301
                        squares); negative where the surface is below that river level
Lookup:    app/elevation.py answers with the lowest value of each file in the 3 x 3 squares around a spot (about
           90 m across; square centres within about 45 m), since a shop's own square is mostly its roof. The files
           themselves hold every square as built.
Checked:   every value read back through app/raster.py byte for byte as written; the surface crop byte for byte equal
           to the source tiles' heights rounded to 0.1 m (decoded a second way with numpy, 4 Oct 2026); hills against
           OpenStreetMap peak heights (Bukit Sapu Tangan 204 m: 216.8; Bukit Nibung Gila 120 m: 118.6; Bukit Lipat
           Kajang 64 m: 65.2; trees add a few metres); river levels in the Taman Sri Muda view 2.0 to 7.3 m, median 2.7;
           the nearest river square right at 3,000 random squares against a search of every river square.
