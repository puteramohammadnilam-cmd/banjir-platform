Johor elevation crops in this folder
====================================

johor_surface.tif and johor_above_river.tif, made by tools/build_elevation.py (from 11_Platform_Draft):

  .venv/bin/python tools/fetch_state_osm.py --area johor rivers
  .venv/bin/python tools/build_elevation.py build --name johor --bbox 102.46 1.26 104.55 2.84 \
      --raw ../05_Raw_Data_Sources/copernicus_glo30 --step 3

Box: Johor's 102.46-104.55 E, 1.26-2.84 N plus 2 km. Squares of 3 arc seconds (about 92 m), 1,939 rows by 2,551
columns: each holds the LOWEST of the nine Copernicus GLO-30 heights inside it, so most roofs and trees drop out, as the
3 x 3 window does on Shah Alam's 1 arc second crops (app/elevation.py reads a square of these on its own). Heights to
0.1 m, float32, no data -9999, read back byte for byte when written. above_river is that surface minus the level of the
nearest mapped OpenStreetMap river or canal square within 3 km (the 10th percentile of the river squares' surface within
150 m), no data beyond, negatives kept. A surface model, not bare earth, and good to a few metres at best.

Low ground map layer
====================

tools/build_lowground.py reads the above_river crop above and writes one indexed PNG per level (1, 2, 3
and 5 m) on its squares, with a JSON of the levels, tones, bounds and these hashes. Each square takes the
height above the river of its own 92 m square, already the lowest of the nine GLO-30 heights in it; a
square at or below the level is shaded in four tones (up to 1 m darkest, then 1-2, 2-3 and 3-5 m), the
rest and no data (beyond 3 km of a mapped river) are transparent. app/lowground.py serves them for the
map. Not a flood forecast or extent: banks, bunds, drains and pumps are left out.
